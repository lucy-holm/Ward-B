One pill. Two realities. Neither tells you everything.

You are a patient in Ward B. Getting out means learning which version of the ward to trust, and when.

Unmedicated, you can read the scrawls and see the orderlies coming. Take your pill and the machinery makes sense, but the people patrolling the ward slip out of sight. Lucidity runs out. You can carry one pill. Find the next dispenser before you need it.

Explore 20 rooms of first-person survival horror and puzzles: match shape seals, follow call-bell sequences, hunt for a missing fuse, decipher codes and work out a route through moving gates. Furnished treatment bays, observation wards and service rooms give each puzzle a different place to unfold. Later on, the reality that felt safe starts making demands of its own.

There is no combat. Use clues, cover and timing to get one door further. Catches return you to the current room; clinical checkpoints let you resume at milestones when your browser supports saved progress.

Choose a grainy, PS1-inspired color style or optional monochrome. Shape symbols and interaction cues remain readable without relying on color. Play free in your browser with mouse and keyboard or touch controls.

This is an evolving playtest. Tell us which room you reached, where you felt lost, and whether the pressure felt fair.

Controls

Desktop: WASD or arrow keys to move, mouse/trackpad to look, E to interact, Q to shift state, Esc to release the cursor or pause. Drag to look where cursor capture is unavailable.

Touch: left stick to move, right-side drag to look, and on-screen interact, shift and pause buttons.

Use Configuration to choose black and white and adjust brightness. Raise brightness until nearby floors, walls and obstacles are clear; it also reveals more detail in unmedicated shadows.

Playtest data

The itch build collects gameplay and technical data to help us understand progress, catches, quitting points and performance. This includes session/player identifiers, approximate in-room positions, and browser/device context. It does not request your name or email.
